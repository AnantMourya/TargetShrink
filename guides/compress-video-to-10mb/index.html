The Mathematics of Video Bitrate: Why Single-Pass CRF Encoding Fails

Most free video converters ask you to select a "Quality level" or rely on single-pass Constant Rate Factor (CRF) encoding (such as CRF 23 or CRF 28).

CRF cannot guarantee an exact target file size. CRF operates by maintaining a constant visual quality level throughout the video. If your video is a talking head clip with a static background, CRF produces a tiny file. If your video is high-action gameplay footage (such as Valorant, Fortnite, or Rocket League) with rapid camera movement, explosions, and particle effects, CRF will generate massive bitrate surges, resulting in a 14MB file that Discord rejects!

To guarantee that a clip lands strictly under 10MB or 25MB, TargetCompress applies two-pass variable bitrate (VBR) mathematics:

Total Allowed Bits = Target Size in Megabytes × 8,388,608 bits
Total Bitrate (bps) = Total Allowed Bits / Duration in Seconds
Target Video Bitrate = Total Bitrate - Audio Bitrate (128 kbps) - Container Overhead (2%)

For example, if you have a 45-second gaming clip that must fit under 10MB:

  • Total bits available: $10 imes 8,388,608 = 83,886,080 ext{ bits}$.
  • Total bitrate ceiling: $83,886,080 / 45 ext{ seconds} pprox 1,864 ext{ kbps}$.
  • Subtracting 128 kbps for clean stereo AAC audio and 2% MP4 container overhead yields a target video bitrate of approximately 1,700 kbps.

Frequently Asked Questions (Video Compression)

Why does video compression take longer than image compression?

A 30-second video at 30 frames per second contains 900 individual frames. To compress it, FFmpeg must decode, motion-compensate, transform, and encode 900 frames in sequence. Because processing happens locally on your computer or phone's CPU rather than on a remote server farm, conversion speed depends on your device's core count. Modern laptops typically encode 60 seconds of video in 20–40 seconds.

The Exact Mathematical Bitrate Formula for 10MB Video Caps

To guarantee that a video file never breaches a 10MB ceiling (such as Discord free tier, WhatsApp attachments, or Slack channel limits), compression cannot rely on vague presets like "medium quality" or "70% compression". It requires calculating total bitrate based on container duration:

Target Total Bitrate (kbps) = (Target Size in Kilobits) / Duration in Seconds

For 10 MB = 10 × 8,192 kilobits = 81,920 kilobits.
Safe Allocation (with 5% container overhead) = 77,824 kilobits.

Video Bitrate = (77,824 / Duration) - Audio Bitrate

For example, if your smartphone clip is 60 seconds long and you encode audio at a standard high-clarity 128 kbps stereo AAC rate:

Video Bitrate = (77,824 / 60) - 128 = 1,297 - 128 = 1,169 kbps.

At 1,169 kbps with modern H.264 / AVC or H.265 / HEVC encoding, a 720p or 1080p video retains crisp motion vectors, fluid 30 frames per second playback, and crystal-clear audio while remaining securely within the 9.8MB safe zone.
